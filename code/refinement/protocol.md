# Confirmation of the filter with a midpoint trajectory
After development (scenes 2000–2011 only), the linear 96x96 variant is frozen, with velocity estimated from two instants, midpoint backtrace and an eight-coefficient spectral filter, strength 0.5. The weights are those computed before the choice, on seeds 1000–1047. Development had identified this variant as the one with the largest error reduction against references of similar cost.
New test of 64 sequences, seeds 6000–6063; the first 32 with wind in the development range, the other 32 beyond that range. Forty frames per scene. No weight updates or parameter choices with these scenes. Reference 144x144 with four substeps. All methods include advection, diffusion, buoyancy, injection and conversion of the density to 144x144. The per-frame step is timed on CPU including the output interpolation, with the order of the methods randomised per scene and one warm-up round.
Comparisons: limited MacCormack, linear and cubic transport, midpoint trajectory and velocity extrapolation without filter. The intermediate grids 104, 112 and 128 avoid too coarse a comparison between costs. The earlier filter and the exact ablation of the new filter are also included. The best comparable classical method is the one with the lowest RMSE among the classical methods with a median time <=115% of the learned method. Primary metric: mean density RMSE per scene over all frames. Positive outcome if the reduction is at least 5%, the bootstrap interval stratified by scene is entirely positive, and the method is at least 2 times faster than the fine reference. Results are reported separately per regime, with the gradient error and the P95 times. The conclusions are limited to the synthetic, periodic 2D benchmark.
