# Spectral operator built into the transport
The learned variant replaces the two-pass MacCormack transport with a single linear interpolation and a multiplicative correction in Fourier space. The correction uses the transforms already required by the diffusion, with coefficients learned from smoke trajectories of the 144x144 reference with four substeps. The new step changes density and vorticity and feeds the next state.
The corrector is a two-field spectral operator layer, conditioned on the statistics of the subpixel displacement. It has six trainable coefficients per field, estimated with regularised quadratic regression. The response is bounded for stability and preserves the zero mode. We evaluate grids 64 and 96. The settings (grid and filter strength) are selected only on the 12 validation scenes, among the variants within the median time of the classical 96x96 MacCormack on validation. If none qualifies, the lowest latency is chosen.
The classical comparisons include limited MacCormack at 48/64/96/144, linear interpolation without correction and limited cubic interpolation, both at 64/96/144. Same time step and same 144x144 rendering grid. The linear comparisons reuse the same code as the learned variant with the filter switched off. The cubic method bounds the density between zero and the field maximum before injection. The sources of all methods are evaluated during the measurement.
New test: 24 scenes with seeds 4000-4023, half with wind beyond the training range. We measure the mean RMSE over the full 40-frame rollout and the CPU latency per frame, order permuted per scene. Operational criterion: at least 5% less error than the best classical method within 115% of the model's latency, a positive paired bootstrap interval, and at least 2 times faster than the fine reference. The outcomes of the first version and of the new test are kept separate.
